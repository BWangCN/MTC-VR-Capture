How these fits were made

Fit

  • No marker names were needed. A start pose was fitted in the quietest half second of each take, with every point about 9 mm off the skin. Each marker was then anchored to its nearest vertex, and the 4 headset markers to the head.
  • Frame by frame, observed points were matched to the predicted anchors, and the pose was updated from the previous frame. All frames were then refined together with temporal smoothing.
  • Anchors were re-checked against each marker's own motion. A left-hand marker that hung next to the hip in the start pose moved from the hip to the hand this way.
  • Body shape and marker offsets were re-estimated over all frames, then the final fit was run.

Placement and scale

The fit is true size, in metres. In the course view the body stands where VR showed it: same alignment and the same player scale Unity applied (×0.877 for the first two takes, ×0.823 for the rest, headset at 1.30 m). “True size” shows the body unscaled on the lab floor.

“Body into obstacles” is how deep the reduced body mesh reaches into the gates and objects at that placement.

Limits

  • SMPL-X neutral with 16 shape coefficients and a VPoser pose prior. Hands are flat and the face is neutral, since there are no finger or face markers.
  • Residuals measure agreement with the markers, not anatomical accuracy. There is no ground truth.
  • The 43 body markers are anonymous anchors, numbered per take.
  • The ScanNet++ room is not on this page because it may not be redistributed. The course objects, gates and route are.
